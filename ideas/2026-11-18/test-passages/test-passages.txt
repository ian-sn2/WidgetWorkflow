TEST 5: long paragraph pacing (45 seconds)

What the voice was given:
Hypoglossal nerve (CN 12): The hypoglossal nerve is a purely motor nerve that innervates the tongue from underneath. This nerve allows for voluntary movement of the tongue and provides constant tone so that the tongue maintains its shape. Its rootlets emerge between the pyramids and the olives on the ventral medulla, where they join to form the hypoglossal nerve. It is the prime mover of the visible part of the tongue, the portion you use for chewing and speech, whereas CN 9 and CN 10 handle the deeper tongue and throat muscles used in swallowing. If one side loses its tone, the halves no longer pull evenly and the tongue drifts to one side instead of protruding straight. A quick clinical check is to ask someone to stick out their tongue: if it veers off-center, suspect the hypoglossal nerve or the brainstem nucleus that houses its cell bodies.
