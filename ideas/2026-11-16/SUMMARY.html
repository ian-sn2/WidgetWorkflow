Three ways to add video, animation and less scrolling to the study guides

Research day, 16 November 2026. Built on the three Exam 3 modules in SentToClaude.zip (MAN4504 Project Management, MAN4504 Aggregate Planning, MAR3023 Chapter 22).

Bottom line. All three ideas are working demos on this exam's own pages, none of them is a quiz, and each is produced by a script from files we already make every semester. The fastest win is the Play button: it needs no change to any existing widget. The largest effect on scrolling is the depth switch, which opens a chapter 60% shorter on a phone with the full text one tap away.

IDEA 1

Play: every step-by-step widget becomes a narrated animation, and a video

A Play button on the widgets we already build. It advances the steps by itself, reads each caption aloud, and animates what changed (the node pulses, the arrow draws in, the new number lands). The same page films itself into an MP4.

IDEA 2

What-if lab: students change the problem and watch the answer move

Tap an activity on the project network to make it run a week late, or to crash it. Early and late times, slack, the critical path, the finish date and the crash cost all update at once, with a sentence explaining why.

It reads the durations and crash costs from the chapter's own tables, so a new example needs no new work. The build stops if its answers differ from the ones the chapter prints.

IDEA 3

Depth switch: Concise, Balanced and Full in one page

The page opens on the short text. A Short / Medium / Full bar changes the whole page, and a "More detail" link under any section opens only that section, without losing your place. It also shows the reading time.

The two resources, summarized

Video: when and how to use Fable 5.2 (Ben, AI agency founder). His claims: Fable 5.2 costs about twice Opus 5.5 and used roughly 70% more in his three side-by-side tests, with clearly better visual and design work and fewer number mistakes. Use it for large, open-ended jobs you hand over and walk away from, and Opus 5.5 for daily work. His eight rules: do not switch models mid-chat; set effort deliberately (default high); give the whole task up front with job, why, guardrails and "done"; ask for progress updates; ask for plain, short reports; add the "finish the work" text; audit old cautious rules; ask for proof and sources, not reasoning.

Anthropic's Fable 5.2 prompting guide. The same points from the source: effort level is the main dial, with most of the gain at high. The model follows rules literally, so a rule containing "never" is treated as absolute and exceptions must be written out. Old notes and cautions pile up and should be pruned. Long tasks need a line asking for updates and a "finish the whole task" block. Final reports should be written for a reader who saw none of the work.

What this means for us. Today's brief already followed the job / why / guardrails / done pattern, and it worked: one message produced all of this. Two follow-ups are worth an hour: prune the project instruction file, which has grown long and is full of absolute rules, and keep the builders' habit of stopping when a number does not match its source, which is the "ask for proof" rule in practice.

Other capabilities we are not using yet

  • Scheduled, unattended runs. A new chapter dropped in a folder can be converted, checked on a phone-size screen and delivered overnight. This suits Fable; day-to-day edits suit a cheaper model.
  • Ask-this-chapter tutor. A chat box that answers only from the chapter, with the section it used. It needs a small server and an API key, so it is a product decision, not a page add-on.
  • Audio edition. The Concise text read as a 5 to 8 minute listen per chapter, from the same text-to-speech step as Idea 1.
  • Tap-to-define terms. The Glossary is already in every Concise file; the same definitions can pop up on any bold term, so explanations need not be repeated in the text.
  • Short vertical clips. The recorder in Idea 1 takes a size, so the same widget can be filmed 9:16 for social posts.

Limits and one finding

  • The YouTube page itself could not be opened from this session; the video summary is based on its spoken content, which was supplied in the chat.
  • An offline voice could not be installed here, so narration was checked for timing and controls but not listened to.
  • Found while measuring: the posted Balanced and Full Aggregate Planning pages scroll sideways by 340 px on a phone because of one wide table. Not changed.
