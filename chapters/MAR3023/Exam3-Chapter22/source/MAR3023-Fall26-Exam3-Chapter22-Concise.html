Developing Timetables

  • PERT chart (Program Evaluation and Review Technique): Best tool for scheduling complex processes; nodes = milestones, lines = tasks, numbers = duration in months
  • Critical path: The longest path from start to finish; determines minimum launch time; focus speed-up efforts here (bottleneck)
  • Gantt chart: Specifies task, responsible person, deadline, and deliverable

EXAM TIP: Interpreting PERT Charts

Expect an exam question requiring PERT chart interpretation. The critical path is the longest path. Speeding up a non-critical path only adds slack — it does not reduce total launch time.

PERT Chart Example:

PERT Chart: New Product Launch

  • \(A \to B \to D \to F = 12\) months
  • \(A \to B \to E \to F = 11\) months
  • \(A \to C \to E \to F = 15\) months* ← Critical Path

Total launch time: 15 months. To shorten, reduce time on A, C, E, or F only.

Resource Allocation — Share Point Analysis

  • Share point: 1% of total industry sales
  • Gross margin: Sales revenue − Cost of goods sold (contribution to fixed costs and profit)
  • 1.5 rule: Increasing market share by 1% requires a 1.5% increase in share of voice

Share Point Analysis Steps

  1. Estimate industry sales (syndicated research or trade publications)
  2. Compute firm market share: \[\text{Market share} = \frac{\text{Firm sales}}{\text{Industry sales}}\]
  3. Compute gross margin per share point: \[\text{Gross margin per share point} = \frac{\text{Gross margin}}{\text{Share points}}\]
  4. Apply 1.5 rule to find required voice point increase
  5. Compute added marketing expense; compare to added gross margin

Share Point Analysis — Worked Example

  • Industry sales: $100B; Firm sales: $15B; Gross margin: $5B
  • Market share: \(\frac{\$15B}{\$100B} = 15\%\) → 15 share points
  • Gross margin per share point: \(\frac{\$5B}{15} = \$333.3M\)
  • Goal: reach 18% → gain 3 share points → +$1B gross margin
  • 1.5 rule: 3 share points × 1.5 = 4.5 voice points needed
  • Industry marketing spend: $10B → $100M per voice point
  • Added expense: 4.5 × $100M = $450M
  • Net gain: $1B − $450M = $550M

Planning vs. Implementation

Plan QualityImplementation QualityOutcome
GoodGoodSuccess
GoodPoorFixable (focus on execution)
PoorGoodFixable (go back to plan)
PoorPoorFailure
  • Planning > implementation in importance: plan defines product benefits, goals, metrics, and contingency responses
  • Good plan + poor execution is easier to fix than poor plan + good execution
  • Marketing strategy: how to achieve a goal (target market + marketing program)
  • Marketing tactics: day-to-day operational decisions executing the strategy

The Control Phase

  • Control (evaluation) phase: measure results, identify deviations, take corrective action
  • Management by exception: focus attention on significant deviations from the plan
  • If results fall short → corrective action; if results exceed goals → double down

Measuring Results

MethodFocusUse
Sales analysisActual sales vs. goalsIdentify regional/segment strengths and weaknesses; shift resources
Profitability analysisProfit by product, segment, territory, channel, order sizeDecide to expand, maintain, reduce, or eliminate offerings

Marketing Audit and ROI Tools

  • Marketing audit: comprehensive, unbiased, periodic review of the strategic marketing process; determines marketing ROI; often conducted by outside consultants for objectivity
  • Marketing ROI: measures what marketing spending accomplishes and quantifies it
  • Marketing metrics: pre-identified quantified benchmarks (e.g., sales goals, retention rates, sales per sq. ft.)
  • Marketing dashboards: real-time displays of key metrics for rapid trend/problem detection

Corrective Action and Contingency Plans

  • Corrective action: taken when actual performance deviates from goals
  • Contingency plans: pre-established responses to scenarios when programs underperform; written into the annual plan

Key Takeaways

  • Expect an exam question requiring PERT chart interpretation — always identify the longest path as the critical path.
  • The critical path is the bottleneck: only reducing time on critical-path activities shortens total launch time.
  • Steps on non-critical paths can run in parallel; speeding them up only adds slack.
  • Share point analysis compares gross margin gained per share point against marketing expense required (via the 1.5 rule).
  • The 1.5 rule: every 1% increase in market share requires a 1.5% increase in share of voice.
  • Gross margin = Sales revenue − Cost of goods sold.
  • If added marketing expense exceeds added gross margin, the investment is not worthwhile.
  • Planning carries more weight than implementation: the plan defines goals, metrics, and contingency responses.
  • Good plan + poor implementation is easier to fix than poor plan + good implementation.
  • Program champions exist at all levels, push both strong and weak ideas, and are defined by determination, not guaranteed success.
  • Avoid paralysis by analysis — at some point additional planning adds no value and delays action.
  • Management by exception directs attention to significant deviations from the plan during the control phase.
  • Marketing audits are most objective when conducted by outside consultants.
  • Marketing dashboards provide real-time metric displays; marketing metrics are pre-set benchmarks for performance judgment.
  • Corrective action and contingency plans should be pre-written into the annual plan to avoid reactive scrambling.
  • Profitability analysis guides expand/maintain/reduce/eliminate decisions across products, segments, and channels.
  • Category manager role: manages an entire product line (broader than a single brand manager).
  • Matrix organization combines market-based and product line groupings.
