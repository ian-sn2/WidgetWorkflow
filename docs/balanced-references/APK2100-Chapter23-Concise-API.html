Widest part of alimentary canal; first site of protein digestion; food stored ~4 hours; produces chyme.

Accessory Organs: The Liver, Gallbladder, and Pancreas

The Liver and Gallbladder

  • Liver: largest gland; produces bile (emulsifies fats); 4 lobes (right, left, quadrate, caudate)
  • Hepatocytes: epithelial-derived; regenerate rapidly
  • Gallbladder: muscular sac on underside of right lobe; stores bile; contains rugae; green color from bile

Bile duct pathway: Left/right hepatic ducts → common hepatic duct + cystic duct → common bile duct → major duodenal papilla → duodenum

  • When sphincter closed, bile backs up → cystic duct → gallbladder for storage
  • Ligamentum teres hepatis: divides left lobe medial/lateral
  • Falciform ligament: liver → anterior abdominal wall (ventral mesentery)
  • Gallstone at major duodenal papilla blocks pancreatic enzymes → dangerous; gallstone at cystic duct → painful but less dangerous

The Pancreas

  • Exocrine: produces majority of digestive enzymes → released into small intestine
  • Endocrine: insulin + glucagon (blood glucose regulation)
  • Anatomy: head (nestled in duodenal C-curve), body, tail (against spleen)
  • Pancreatic duct → major duodenal papilla (shared with common bile duct)
  • Accessory duct: backup route to duodenum if primary blocked
  • Retroperitoneal (except tail)

The Large Intestine

Receives unabsorbed material; major function: reabsorbs water and electrolytes; chyme enters liquid → feces exits solid.

Motion: mass peristaltic movement (infrequent); triggered by cecal distension.

Gross Anatomy

Path: Ileum → ileocecal valve → cecum → ascending colon → right colic (hepatic) flexure → transverse colon → left colic (splenic) flexure → descending colon → sigmoid colon → rectum → anal canal → anus

The Rectum, Anus, and Anal Sphincters

  • Rectum: retroperitoneal; contains anal columns (longitudinal ridges) and rectal valves (allow gas, retain solid)
  • Pectinate line: distinct line at distal anal canal; divides hemorrhoid types
  • Anus: transition from simple columnar → keratinized stratified squamous epithelium (mucous → cutaneous membrane)
SphincterMuscle TypeControl
Internal anal sphincterSmooth (thickened circular muscularis)Involuntary
External anal sphincterSkeletalVoluntary
  • Levator ani muscle: lifts anus; facilitates smooth defecation
  • Defecation reflex: rectal distension → spinal reflex → rectal wall contracts + internal sphincter relaxes; voluntary inhibition of external sphincter allows defecation

Clinical Correlation: Hemorrhoids

Hemorrhoid: swollen/varicose vein in anal mucosa/submucosa.

  • External: inferior to pectinate line; very painful; minimal bleeding
  • Internal: superior to pectinate line; less painful; bleeds significantly

Treatment: rubber-band ligation (cuts blood supply → shrinks). Bright red blood = distal bleeding (e.g., hemorrhoids); black/tarry stool = proximal/upper GI bleeding.

Key Takeaways

  • Know all structures of the alimentary canal wall diagram — expect image-based identification questions.
  • Papillae are NOT taste buds; papillae visible, taste buds microscopic.
  • Stomach muscularis has 3 layers (longitudinal, circular, oblique); all other GI segments have 2.
  • Stomach rugae are temporary; plicae circulares are permanent.
  • Chief cells → pepsinogen; parietal cells → HCl; together → pepsin.
  • Duodenum, ascending colon, descending colon, rectum, pancreas = retroperitoneal; use SAD PUCKER.
  • GI intraperitoneal/retroperitoneal alternates stomach → rectum; jejunum+ileum count as one unit.
  • Upper 1/3 esophagus = skeletal muscle (voluntary); lower 1/3 = smooth only (involuntary).
  • Cardiac sphincter failure → GERD; pyloric sphincter = sphincter, NOT a true valve.
  • Ileocecal valve closes by stretch/distension of cecum (not a standard sphincter).
  • Large intestine uses mass peristaltic movement (infrequent), not constant peristalsis.
  • Internal anal sphincter = smooth/involuntary; external anal sphincter = skeletal/voluntary.
  • External hemorrhoids (below pectinate line) = painful, minimal bleed; internal (above) = less pain, significant bleed.
  • Bright red rectal blood = distal source; black/tarry stool = upper GI source.
  • Gallstone at major duodenal papilla = dangerous (blocks pancreatic enzymes); at cystic duct = painful but less dangerous.
  • Greater omentum = dorsal mesentery despite being most ventral structure in abdomen.
  • Genioglossus = extrinsic tongue muscle; reduced tone → airway obstruction → sleep apnea.
  • Urinary bladder normal position = hypogastric only; superior extension = pathological overdistension.
  • Accessory organs (liver, pancreas, salivary glands) are not part of the alimentary canal tube.
  • Pancreas: exocrine (digestive enzymes) + endocrine (insulin/glucagon); retroperitoneal except tail.
