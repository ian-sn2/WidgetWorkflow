Question 1 · The PPF

All points on the PPF are

  1. production efficient.
  2. allocatively efficient.
  1. Both i and ii are correct.
  2. Only i is correct.
  3. Only ii is correct.
  4. Neither i not ii are correct.
Solution

(b) A point is production efficient when the economy cannot make more of one good unless it makes less of another. That description fits every point on the PPF, because the PPF marks the outer limit of what the nation can produce with its available resources and technology. Statement i is therefore correct.

Allocative efficiency is a tougher standard. It requires the economy to produce the one combination of goods that society values most highly, which is the combination for which MSB = MSC. Only a single point on the PPF meets that condition, so it is not true that all points on the PPF are allocatively efficient, and statement ii is incorrect. Because only statement i holds, the answer is (b).

Question 2 · Elasticity

When the price of a pizza is $21, the quantity of pizza demanded is 46. When the price is $19, the quantity demanded is 54. The price elasticity of demand between these two points is equal to

  1. 4.00.
  2. 0.25.
  3. 1.60.
  4. 0.625.
  5. None of the above answers are correct.
Solution

(c) The price elasticity of demand tells us how strongly the quantity demanded responds to a change in price. It is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price, stated as an absolute value (that is, ignoring the minus sign):

    ED = %ΔQD / %ΔP

When we are given two points on a demand curve, each percentage change is found with the midpoint formula, which divides the change by the average of the two values. The average quantity is (46 + 54) ÷ 2 = 50 pizzas, and the average price is ($21 + $19) ÷ 2 = $20.

    %ΔQD = (54 − 46) / 50 = 8 / 50 = 0.16 = 16%

    %ΔP = ($19 − $21) / $20 = −$2 / $20 = −0.10 = −10%

Dropping the minus sign and dividing:

    ED = 16% / 10% = 1.60

The price elasticity of demand between these two points is 1.60. Because ED > 1, demand is elastic over this range. Be careful not to flip the fraction: dividing the price change by the quantity change (10% ÷ 16%) gives 0.625, which is answer (d) and is incorrect.

Question 3 · Supply and Demand

Potato chips are a normal good and people’s incomes do not change however the price of potatoes rises. As a result, the equilibrium price of a pound of potato chips ____ and the equilibrium quantity ____.

  1. might rise, fall, or not change; increases
  2. falls; decreases
  3. falls; might increase, decrease, or not change
  4. rises; decreases
  5. None of the above answers are correct.
Solution

(d) Potatoes are an input used to make potato chips, so a higher price of potatoes means a higher cost of producing potato chips. A rise in the cost of production decreases supply, which shifts the supply curve for potato chips to the left.

The statement that potato chips are a normal good is a distraction here. Income matters only when it changes, and we are told that incomes stay the same, so the demand curve does not move. With the supply curve shifting leftward along an unchanged demand curve, the equilibrium price of potato chips rises and the equilibrium quantity decreases.

Question 4 · Elasticity

The price elasticity of demand for streaming movies is 1.50 and the price of streaming increases by 3 percent. Consequently, the quantity demanded of streaming movies decreases by

  1. 4.50 percent.
  2. 6.50 percent.
  3. 2.00 percent.
  4. 0.50 percent.
  5. None of the numeric answers is correct.
Solution

(a) The price elasticity of demand is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price, stated as an absolute value:

    ED = %ΔQD / %ΔP

In this question we know that ED = 1.50 and that %ΔP = 3%. The unknown is %ΔQD, so we rearrange the formula by multiplying both sides by %ΔP:

    %ΔQD = ED × %ΔP = 1.50 × 3% = 4.50%

The law of demand says that price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions. Since the price of streaming went up, the quantity demanded goes down, and it does so by 4.50 percent.

Question 5 · Supply and Demand

When a barrel of oil is refined, gasoline and diesel are simultaneously produced. Suppose the demand for gasoline decreases. As a result, the equilibrium price of diesel ____ and the equilibrium quantity of diesel ____.

  1. rises; increases
  2. rises; decreases
  3. falls; increases
  4. falls; decreases
  5. does not change; does not change
Solution

(b) Gasoline and diesel come out of the same barrel of oil at the same time, which makes them complements in production: producing more of one automatically means producing more of the other, and producing less of one means producing less of the other.

Work through this in two steps. First, in the gasoline market, the decrease in demand shifts the demand curve for gasoline to the left, so the equilibrium price and quantity of gasoline both fall. Second, because less gasoline is being produced, fewer barrels of oil are being refined, and so less diesel is produced as well. This is a decrease in the supply of diesel, shown as a leftward shift of the supply curve for diesel. Nothing has happened to the demand for diesel, so the equilibrium price of diesel rises and the equilibrium quantity of diesel decreases.

Question 6 · Efficiency and Equity

Adam Smith’s “invisible hand” idea concludes that

  1. the fairest price is the equilibrium price.
  2. the equilibrium quantity is the same as the efficient quantity.
  3. too much of some goods are produced and too little of other goods are produced.
  4. people pursuing their self-interest leads to a deadweight loss.
Solution

(b) Adam Smith's "invisible hand" is the idea that when buyers and sellers in a competitive market each look out for their own self-interest, they are guided, as though by an unseen hand, to an outcome that is best for society as a whole. In the language of this course, the competitive market's equilibrium quantity is the same as the efficient quantity: the quantity where MSB = MSC, total surplus is as large as possible, and there is no deadweight loss.

The other choices do not fit. Answer (a) is about fairness, but the invisible hand is a statement about efficiency and says nothing about what is fair. Answers (c) and (d) both describe an inefficient outcome, which is the opposite of what the invisible hand concludes.

Question 7 · Supply and Demand

Suppose both demanders and suppliers become convinced that the price of oil will be lower in a month. As a result, the price of oil today ____ and the quantity today ____.

  1. rises; increases
  2. falls; increases
  3. might rise, fall, or stay the same; increases
  4. falls; might increase, decrease, or stay the same
  5. might rise, fall, or not change; increases
Solution

(d) This is a case in which both the demand and supply curves shift:

  • Buyers who expect oil to be cheaper next month put off some of their purchases until then, so the demand for oil today decreases. On its own, a leftward shift of the demand curve lowers both the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity.
  • Sellers who expect oil to be cheaper next month would rather sell now, while the price is still relatively high, so the supply of oil today increases. On its own, a rightward shift of the supply curve lowers the equilibrium price and raises the equilibrium quantity.

Both shifts push the price in the same direction, so the price of oil today definitely falls. The two shifts push the quantity in opposite directions, however: the demand shift reduces it and the supply shift raises it. Without knowing which shift is bigger, we cannot say what happens to the quantity. It might increase, decrease, or stay the same.

Question 8 · Elasticity

If the elasticity of demand for a good x and good y with respect to income are both positive, then based on this the goods are

  1. normal goods.
  2. inferior goods.
  3. complements.
  4. substitutes.
Solution

(a) The income elasticity of demand measures how the demand for a good responds to a change in income. It is the percentage change in the quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income:

    Ey = %ΔQD / %ΔIncome

If this number is positive, income and demand move in the same direction, meaning people buy more of the good when their incomes rise. That is the definition of a normal good. If the number is negative, people buy less of the good when their incomes rise, and the good is an inferior good. Both good x and good y have a positive income elasticity, so both are normal goods.

Whether two goods are complements or substitutes is determined by the cross elasticity of demand, not the income elasticity. We have no information about the cross elasticity here, so answers (c) and (d) cannot be concluded.

Question 9 · Elasticity

Suppose that a 20% fall in the price of a good increases the quantity demanded by 10%. From this we can tell that the demand is ____ and the total revenue from sales of the good ___.

  1. elastic; increases
  2. elastic; decreases
  3. inelastic; decreases
  4. inelastic; increases
  5. inelastic; might increase, decrease, or not change
Solution

(c) Start with the elasticity. The price elasticity of demand is the absolute value of the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price:

    ED = %ΔQD / %ΔP = 10% / 20% = 0.5

Because ED < 1, demand is inelastic.

Now apply the total revenue test. Total revenue equals price multiplied by quantity. When demand is inelastic, the percentage change in quantity is smaller than the percentage change in price, so the price change dominates and total revenue moves in the same direction as the price. Here the price falls by 20% while the quantity sold rises by only 10%. The additional units sold are not enough to make up for the lower price on every unit, so total revenue decreases.

A quick example shows the same thing. Suppose the price falls from $10 to $8 (a 20% drop) and the quantity rises from 100 to 110 units (a 10% increase). Total revenue goes from $10 × 100 = $1,000 to $8 × 110 = $880, which is a decrease.

Question 10 · Government Intervention

Increasing opportunity cost while moving along a production possibilities frontier is the result of

  1. taxes.
  2. firms’ needs to earn profits.
  3. the fact that it is more difficult to use resources efficiently the more society produces.
  4. the fact that resources are not equally productive in alternative uses.
Solution

(d) Opportunity cost rises as we move along the PPF because resources are not equally productive in every use. Some workers, land, and equipment are well suited to producing one good and poorly suited to producing the other. When a nation first expands production of a good, it can shift over the resources that are best suited to that good, and it gives up very little of the other good. As it keeps expanding, it has to draw on resources that are less and less suited to the task, so each additional unit requires giving up more of the other good than the unit before. This rising opportunity cost is the reason the PPF is bowed outward.

Taxes and firms' profits play no role in the shape of the PPF, which rules out (a) and (b). Answer (c) is also incorrect, because every point on the PPF is production efficient regardless of how much is being produced.

Question 11 · Supply and Demand

Hot dogs are a substitute for hamburgers and hot dogs rise in price. As a result, the price of a hamburger ____ and the quantity of hamburgers ____.

  1. rises; increases
  2. rises; decreases
  3. falls; increases
  4. falls; decreases
  5. does not change; increases
Solution

(a) Substitutes are goods that can be used in place of one another. When the price of a good rises, the demand for its substitute increases. Here, the higher price of hot dogs leads some consumers to switch to hamburgers, so the demand curve for hamburgers shifts to the right. The supply of hamburgers has not changed. A rightward shift of the demand curve raises both the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity of hamburgers.

Question 12 · Government Intervention

(This is an out-of-the-book question.) Using the “It’s not fair if the result isn’t fair” principle of fairness, an income tax designed to transfer wealth from the rich to the poor

  1. increases efficiency and equity.
  2. increases efficiency and not affect equity.
  3. decreases efficiency and increases equity.
  4. decreases efficiency and equity.
Solution

(c) The "it's not fair if the result isn't fair" principle judges fairness by looking at outcomes, specifically at how evenly income and wealth are spread across people. By that standard, a tax that moves wealth from the rich to the poor makes the distribution more equal and therefore increases equity.

The gain in equity comes at a price, though, which is known as the big tradeoff between efficiency and fairness. Taxing income weakens people's incentive to work and to save, and running the tax-and-transfer system uses up resources of its own. As a result, a dollar collected from a rich person ends up delivering less than a dollar to a poor person, and the economy's total output is smaller than it otherwise would be. The income tax therefore decreases efficiency at the same time that it increases equity.

Question 14 · Elasticity

The demand for the wheat Kisha grows is perfectly elastic. This fact means that the demand curve for Kisha’s wheat is

  1. a downward sloping straight line with a slope of −1.
  2. a rectangular hyperbola.
  3. positively sloped.
  4. vertical.
  5. horizontal.
Solution

(e) When demand is perfectly elastic, the price elasticity of demand is infinite: buyers are so sensitive to price that even the smallest price increase causes the quantity demanded to fall to zero. The demand curve that shows this is a horizontal line at the going price. This makes sense for Kisha. Her wheat is identical to the wheat grown by thousands of other farmers, so buyers will take as much as she wants to sell at the market price but will buy none of it if she asks for even slightly more.

For comparison, a vertical demand curve represents perfectly inelastic demand (ED = 0), where the quantity demanded does not respond to price at all. A rectangular hyperbola is the shape of a demand curve that is unit elastic at every point.

Question 15 · Efficiency and Equity

Suppose the deadweight loss from under-producing a product is $3 billion. This $3 billion equals the

  1. loss to the consumer and the gain to the producer.
  2. loss to the producer and the gain to the consumer.
  3. gain to the consumer and the producer, and a loss to the rest of society.
  4. loss to society.
Solution

(d) A deadweight loss is the reduction in total surplus that occurs when the quantity produced is not the efficient quantity. When a product is under-produced, there are units that are not being made even though their marginal social benefit is greater than their marginal social cost. The surplus those units would have generated is never created. It is not passed from consumers to producers or from producers to consumers, and it does not go to anyone else in society. It simply disappears, which is why the $3 billion is a loss to society as a whole. Answers (a), (b), and (c) all describe surplus being transferred from one group to another, and a transfer is not a deadweight loss.

Question 16 · Supply and Demand

Travel by bus is an inferior good and people’s incomes fall. As a result, the price of bus travel ____ and the quantity ____.

  1. falls; decreases
  2. falls; increases
  3. rises; decreases
  4. rises; increases
Solution

(d) An inferior good is a good for which demand moves in the opposite direction from income: demand decreases when income rises and increases when income falls. Bus travel is an inferior good and incomes fall, so the demand for bus travel increases and the demand curve shifts to the right. The supply curve does not change. As a result, the equilibrium price of bus travel rises and the equilibrium quantity increases.

Be careful not to treat bus travel like a normal good. For a normal good, a fall in income would decrease demand and lead to answer (a).

Question 17 · The PPF

An increase in the nation’s capital stock

  1. shifts the PPF outward.
  2. results in a movement along the PPF upward and leftward.
  3. results in a movement along the PPF downward and rightward.
  4. moves the nation from producing on the PPF to producing at a point within the PPF.
  5. moves the nation from producing within the PPF to producing at a point closer to the PPF.
Solution

(a) Capital refers to the tools, machines, buildings, and other equipment used to produce goods and services. The position of the PPF depends on the quantity of resources and the technology a nation has available. With a larger capital stock, the nation has more resources to work with and can produce more of both goods than before. This is economic growth, and it is shown as an outward shift of the PPF.

A movement along the PPF, as in (b) and (c), represents a change in the mix of goods produced using the same resources. A move between a point inside the PPF and a point on it, as in (d) and (e), has to do with whether existing resources are fully and efficiently used, not with how many resources there are.

Question 20 · Efficiency and Equity

If the price of a good is exactly equal to the maximum price a buyer will pay, then the buyer will

  1. not buy the good.
  2. receive the maximum consumer surplus.
  3. receive no marginal benefit from that unit of the good.
  4. receive no consumer surplus from that unit of the good.
  5. Both answers C and D are correct.
Solution

(d) The consumer surplus on a unit of a good is the marginal benefit from that unit, measured by the maximum price the buyer is willing to pay, minus the price actually paid. If the price is exactly equal to the maximum the buyer will pay, the difference is zero, so the buyer receives no consumer surplus from that unit.

The buyer is still willing to buy the unit, because the price does not exceed what it is worth to them, so (a) is incorrect. Answer (c) is also incorrect: the buyer does receive a marginal benefit from the unit. That marginal benefit is equal to the price; it is not equal to zero. Since (c) is wrong, (e) is wrong too.

Question 22 · Elasticity

If two goods are substitutes, their cross elasticity of demand is

  1. positive.
  2. zero.
  3. negative.
  4. positive when the price of one of them increases and negative when the price of one of them decreases.
Solution

(a) The cross elasticity of demand measures how the demand for one good responds to a change in the price of another good. It is the percentage change in the quantity demanded of one good divided by the percentage change in the price of the other good:

    EX = (%ΔQD of one good) / (%ΔP of the other good)

When two goods are substitutes, a higher price for one leads consumers to switch to the other, so the quantity demanded of the second good rises. The numerator and the denominator have the same sign, which makes the cross elasticity positive. For complements, the two move in opposite directions, so the cross elasticity is negative.

Answer (d) is incorrect because the sign does not depend on whether the price goes up or down. If the price of one substitute falls, the quantity demanded of the other falls too, and a negative number divided by a negative number is still positive.

Question 23 · Elasticity

The price elasticity of demand for food is ____ price elasticity of demand for pizza.

  1. probably larger than
  2. probably equal to
  3. probably less than
  4. not comparable to the
Solution

(c) The most important influence on the price elasticity of demand is how easy it is to find substitutes. The more close substitutes a good has, the more readily buyers switch away when its price rises, and the more elastic its demand is. Question 25 · Supply and Demand

The price of chicken feed (the grain fed to chickens) rises while simultaneously the price of fish, a substitute for chicken falls. Consequently, the price of chickens ____ and the quantity ____.

  2. does not change; decreases
  3. might rise, fall, or not change; decreases

(c) This is a case in which both the demand and supply curves shift:

Question 26 · Efficiency and Equity

The maximum price Katie is willing to pay for a pizza is $19 and the price she actually pays is $15. Her marginal benefit from the pizza is

  1. $19.
  2. $15.
  3. $4.
  4. None of the above answers is correct.
Solution

(a) The marginal benefit of a good is the benefit a person receives from consuming one more unit of it, and we measure it by the maximum price the person is willing to pay for that unit. The most Katie will pay for the pizza is $19, so her marginal benefit from the pizza is $19.

The $15 in answer (b) is the price she pays, which is not the same thing as the value she places on the pizza. The $4 in answer (c) is her consumer surplus, the difference between her marginal benefit and the price: $19 − $15 = $4.

Question 28 · Efficiency and Equity

Which of the following is/are correct?

  1. There is a deadweight loss if less than the allocatively efficient quantity of a product is produced.
  2. There is a deadweight loss if more than the allocatively efficient quantity of a product is produced.
  1. Both i and ii are correct.
  2. Only i is correct.
  3. Only ii is correct.
  4. Neither i not ii are correct.
Solution

(a) The allocatively efficient quantity is the quantity at which MSB = MSC, and it is the quantity that makes total surplus as large as possible. Producing any other quantity, whether smaller or larger, reduces total surplus, and that reduction is the deadweight loss.

If less than the efficient quantity is produced, there are units left unmade for which MSB > MSC. Each of those units would have added to total surplus, so failing to produce them creates a deadweight loss, and statement i is correct. If more than the efficient quantity is produced, the extra units have MSC > MSB. Each of them costs society more than it is worth, so producing them subtracts from total surplus and also creates a deadweight loss, and statement ii is correct. Both statements are correct.

Question 29 · The PPF

Production inefficient points are those points

  1. only on the production possibilities frontier (PPF).
  2. only inside the production possibilities frontier (PPF).
  3. only outside the production possibilities frontier (PPF).
  4. either on or inside the production possibilities frontier (PPF).
  5. either on or outside the production possibilities frontier (PPF).
Solution

(b) Production is inefficient when it would be possible to produce more of one good without producing less of any other. That is the situation at any point inside the PPF, where some resources are either unemployed or not assigned to the tasks they are best suited for. By putting those resources to better use, the nation could move outward toward the PPF and have more of one good, or even more of both.

Points on the PPF are production efficient, because from those points more of one good can be had only by giving up some of the other. Points outside the PPF are not inefficient; they are unattainable, meaning they cannot be produced at all with the nation's current resources and technology. Only the points inside the PPF are production inefficient.

Question 1 · Elasticity

Suppose that a 2% fall in income increases the quantity of used cars demanded by 10%. From this we can tell that used cars

  1. are a normal good.
  2. are an inferior good.
  3. have an elastic demand.
  4. have an inelastic demand.
  5. Both answers B and D are correct.
Solution

(b) This question tests the income elasticity of demand, which measures how strongly the quantity demanded of a good responds to a change in income. It equals the percentage change in the quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income:

\[ E_y = \%\Delta Q_D \ / \ \%\Delta \text{Income} \]

Here income falls by 2% and the quantity of used cars demanded rises by 10%, so:

\[ E_y = +10\% \ / \ {-2\%} = -5 \]

The sign is what matters. A negative income elasticity means that the quantity demanded moves in the opposite direction from income, which is exactly what defines an inferior good. (A normal good has a positive income elasticity.) Answers (c), (d), and (e) can be ruled out because "elastic" and "inelastic" demand describe how buyers respond to a change in the good's own price, and the question gives no information about a price change.

Question 2 · Government Intervention

If the government imposes a tax on pizza companies, the equilibrium quantity of pizza ____ and the efficient quantity of pizza ____.

  1. increases; increases
  2. increases; decreases
  3. decreases; increases
  4. decreases; decreases
  5. None of the above answers are correct.
Solution

(e) A tax imposed on pizza companies works like an increase in their cost of selling pizza, so the supply curve of pizza shifts leftward (upward by the amount of the tax). The price buyers pay rises and the equilibrium quantity decreases. The efficient quantity, however, is the quantity at which MSB = MSC. A tax does not change how much consumers value pizza or the resources needed to make it, so neither the MSB curve nor the MSC curve moves, and the efficient quantity does not change. The blanks should therefore read "decreases; does not change," which is not offered in (a) through (d), so the answer is (e). The gap between the smaller, taxed equilibrium quantity and the unchanged efficient quantity is precisely why a tax creates a deadweight loss.

Question 3 · Supply and Demand

Question 6 · Elasticity

When the price of a pizza is $18, 12 million pizzas per day are demanded. When the price of a pizza is $22, 8 million pizzas per day are demanded. The price elasticity of demand equals

  1. 0.25.
  2. 4.00.
  3. 0.50.
  4. 2.00.
  5. None of the answers is correct.
Solution

(d) The price elasticity of demand is the absolute value of the percentage change in the quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in the price:

\[ E_D = \left| \%\Delta Q_D \ / \ \%\Delta P \right| \]

The percentage changes are calculated with the midpoint formula, which divides each change by the average of the starting and ending values.

Percentage change in quantity demanded:

\[ \%\Delta Q_D = (8 - 12) \ / \ [(12 + 8) \ / \ 2] = -4 \ / \ 10 = -0.40, \text{ or } -40\% \]

Percentage change in price:

\[ \%\Delta P = (\$22 - \$18) \ / \ [(\$18 + \$22) \ / \ 2] = \$4 \ / \ \$20 = 0.20, \text{ or } 20\% \]

Price elasticity of demand:

\[ E_D = \left| -40\% \ / \ 20\% \right| = 2.00 \]

Because ED > 1, demand is elastic over this price range. Be careful not to flip the ratio: dividing the percentage change in price by the percentage change in quantity gives 0.50, which is answer (c) and is incorrect.

Question 9 · Efficiency and Equity

If the demand for a product increases, then the total surplus from the product ____.

  1. rises.
  2. does not change.
  3. falls.
  4. might rise, fall, or not change
Solution

(a) Total surplus is the sum of consumer surplus and producer surplus. On a supply and demand graph, it is the area between the demand curve and the supply curve, from zero out to the equilibrium quantity. An increase in demand shifts the demand curve rightward, which raises both the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity. The area between the new, higher demand curve and the unchanged supply curve, measured out to the new, larger quantity, is bigger than before. Intuitively, the units that were already being produced are now valued more highly by buyers, and additional units whose marginal benefit exceeds their marginal cost are now produced as well. Total surplus rises.

Question 13 · Government Intervention

The government can tax either product A, with a very elastic demand, or product B, with a very inelastic demand. The price elasticity of supply is the same for both products. If the government wants consumers to pay a larger fraction of the tax, the government will

  1. tax product A.
  2. tax product B.
  3. impose half the tax on product A and Half on product B
  4. None of the above answers is correct.
Solution

(b) This question is about tax incidence, which is the division of a tax between buyers and sellers. The side of the market that is less responsive to price changes, that is, the side with the smaller elasticity, ends up paying the larger share of the tax. Because the elasticity of supply is the same for the two products, the only difference is the elasticity of demand. When demand is very inelastic, as it is for product B, buyers continue to purchase nearly the same quantity even when the price rises, so sellers can pass most of the tax on to them through a higher price. To make consumers pay the larger fraction, the government will tax product B.

Question 17 · Government Intervention

  1. decreases; does not change
  2. does not change; decreases
  3. decreases; increases
  4. increases; decreases
Solution

(c) A minimum wage is a price floor in the labor market, and it matters when it is set above the equilibrium wage. Question 18 · Elasticity

  1. positive; increases
  2. negative; decreases
  3. negative; increases
  4. positive; decreases
Solution

(a) The cross elasticity of demand measures how the quantity demanded of one good responds to a change in the price of another good:

Cross elasticity of demand = (%Δ in quantity of coffee demanded) / (%Δ in price of tea)

Question 27 · Efficiency and Equity
